Adyasha Patra

I’m Adyasha Patra, a Master’s student in Computer Science at University of California San Diego, working on improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models using supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. My interests lie in building LLMs that are more reliable, efficient, and consistent on complex reasoning tasks. At UCSD, I work at the Rose-STL Lab with Prof. Rose Yu to enhance the reasoning capabilities of AI models in mathematics by trying to bridge the gap between formal and informal reasoning.

I previously completed my B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering at Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, with an Honors in CSE and a minor in AI & Data Science. Prior to my current work, I focused on multilingual speech and natural language processing at the CSALT Lab, working with Prof. Preethi Jyothi. My work spanned code-switched speech recognition, ASR for pathological speech, and cross-lingual transfer. I also contributed to BharatGen with Prof. Ganesh Ramakrishnan, supporting the development of large-scale multilingual AI systems for Indian languages.

  1. Semantically Cohesive Word Grouping in Indian Languages
    N J Karthika, Adyasha Patra, Nagasai Saketh Naidu, and 3 more authors
    2025
    Abs arXiv PDF

    Indic languages share deep syntactic similarities, yet standard NLP pipelines treat them independently. We propose a semantically cohesive word-grouping strategy that unifies sub-sentential structure across Indian languages for better cross-lingual alignment, and apply it to decomposed machine translation (DecoMT), improving spBLEU and chrF++ across five Hindi-to-Indic language pairs while normalizing Hindi’s outlier word-count tokenization.

  2. Improving Language Identification for Code-Switched Speech: The Pivotal Role of Accented English
    Adyasha Patra, Dhiraj Kumar Sah, and Preethi Jyothi
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL 2026, Mar 2026
    Accepted, Findings of EACL 2026 Abs DOI

    true

    Code-switching, where speakers alternate between languages within a single utterance, poses unique challenges for language identification (LID). Existing LID models often fail to reliably identify English spoken with the accent of the matrix (dominant) language. We show that finetuning LID models with small amounts of such accented English significantly improves code-switched LID, without degrading performance on standard monolingual speech—a limitation observed with direct finetuning on code-switched utterances. This is achieved via low-rank adaptation (LoRA) on limited accented data, which allows models to adapt efficiently. To better evaluate performance, we introduce LangRank, a metric that captures the relative ranking of identified languages often overlooked by traditional metrics. Our method generalizes across multiple language pairs, including Hindi-English, Bengali-English, Mandarin-English, and Arabic-English, providing robust LID in code-switched multilingual contexts.
